# Pedagogical Report

**Role**: Pedagogical Consultant
**Date**: 2024-05-22
**Scope**: Review of instructional design and educational effectiveness for the "Prácticas Profesionalizantes" guide.

## 1. General Pedagogical Assessment
The guide has a strong technical foundation, but there is a noticeable gap between the *delivery of information* and the *acquisition of competence*. The material currently follows a traditional instructional model (Theory $ightarrow$ Exercise), which may not be the most effective approach for 17-18 year old students in a professionalizing context.

## 2. Instructional Design Analysis

### Chapter: Project Planning (Gantt/PERT)
- **Cognitive Load**: The jump in complexity is too abrupt. The students go from simple definitions (Exercise 1) to complex network calculations (Exercise 3) without intermediate scaffolding. This can lead to frustration and disengagement.
- **Time Management**: The volume of work in the activities section (5 complex exercises, including drawing and calculating) is likely to exceed the 160-minute weekly window. Students will either rush the process or fail to complete the tasks in class.
- **Scaffolding**: There is a lack of "Guided Practice". The guide should provide a partially completed example that the student must finish before tackling the independent cases.

### Chapter: Fixed Costs
- **Learning Method**: The activity is designed as a traditional questionnaire ("Investigate and answer"). This is a passive learning strategy that encourages rote memorization rather than critical thinking or application.
- **Alignment with Professional Practices**: For a "Professionalizing Practice" course, the activity should be an application. Instead of asking "What is depreciation?", the student should be asked to "Calculate the annual depreciation of a specific lathe for the project".

## 3. Recommendations for Improvement
- **Implement Scaffolding**: In the Planning chapter, introduce a "Worked Example" section. Show the step-by-step process of a forward and backward pass in a PERT network before asking them to do it.
- **Active Learning Transition**: Transform the Costs questionnaire into a "Cost Analysis Workshop". Provide a list of assets and expenses and ask the students to categorize them and calculate the impact on the project's budget.
- **Time-Boxing**: Break down the activities into "In-Class Tasks" (essential) and "Extension Tasks" (for fast learners) to ensure the 160-minute limit is respected.
- **Assessment Rubrics**: Move away from binary (Correct/Incorrect) grading. Create rubrics that value the *process* of planning and the *logic* behind the cost analysis, not just the final number.

**Conclusion**: The content is technically rich, but the delivery is too traditional. By introducing scaffolding and shifting from passive questionnaires to active application, we can significantly increase student engagement and ensure the learning objectives are met within the available time.
