## 1. Transformation of "Costos Variables"

### A. Contextual Pivot: From Vineyards to Workshops
The current example of a winery is a pedagogical dead-end for an electromechanical student. We need to anchor the concept of "variability" in the physical reality of the workshop.

**Pedagogical Change**: 
- **The "Consumables" Analogy**: Replace the winery example with a **Machine Shop** scenario. Explain variable costs through items that are consumed per piece: welding electrodes, grinding discs, electricity per machine-hour, and raw steel profiles.
- **Integration with Profitability**: Instead of treating variable costs as a separate topic, we will present them as the final piece of the puzzle for the **Break-even Point**. 

### B. Activity Redesign: The "Unit Cost Sheet"
Replace the passive questionnaire with a **"Variable Cost Analysis Workshop"**.

**The New Activity Structure:**
1. **Material Listing**: The student must create a detailed list of all materials and consumables required to produce **one single unit** of their project.
2. **Unit Cost Calculation**: Research current market prices to determine the exact cost of each item.
3. **The Profitability Link**: Using the Fixed Costs calculated in the previous chapter and the new Variable Costs, the student must calculate the **final Break-even Point** for their project.

**Benefit**: The student stops asking "What is a variable cost?" and starts asking "How much does it cost me to build one more unit of my machine?"

## 2. Transformation of "Estudio de Mercado"

### A. Trimming the "Theoretical Fat"
The current chapter reads like a general economics textbook. For a professional practice project, the "Global Market" is irrelevant; the "Local Market" is everything.

**Pedagogical Change**: 
- **Strategic Cut**: Remove the sections on "Global Market" and "Service Markets".
- **Focus on the "Niche"**: Introduce the concept of the **Target User (User Persona)**. Instead of defining "The Market", students will define *who* specifically would buy or use their machine (e.g., "Small-scale artisanal bakers in the city").
- **Practical Supplier Analysis**: Keep the supplier theory but move it immediately into a tool: the **Supplier Comparison Matrix**.

### B. Activity Redesign: The "Market Validation Dossier"
Replace the 9-question investigation list with a **"Market Validation Report"** for their specific project.

**The New Activity Structure:**
1. **The User Profile**: Create a description of the ideal user for the project (needs, pain points, and budget).
2. **The Supplier Matrix**: Identify 3 real suppliers for the project's most critical material. Compare them based on: *Price, Delivery Time, and Technical Support*.
3. **Competitor Mapping**: Identify 2 existing solutions (commercial or DIY) that solve the same problem and list one advantage and one disadvantage of each compared to their proposal.

**Benefit**: This transforms a rote-learning exercise into a real-world validation process. The student proves that their project is not just a school exercise, but a viable product.
